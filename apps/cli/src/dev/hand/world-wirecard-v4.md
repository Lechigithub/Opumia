# Wirecard v4: world bible (build list 6, D3)

Written before any scene, from the story's own things (the channel's rule: a film dresses in its subject's own visual
language and era). Nothing here is taken from FTX or from Wirecard v1–v3.

## What this story is made of (its inventory)
- **A payments company:**
  - card terminals, the moment a card is tapped;
  - thermal paper receipts;
  - the terminal's LCD ("APPROVED", "DECLINED", "PROCESSING");
  - transaction logs, merchant IDs;
  - Wirecard's own lowercase blue wordmark.
- **Aschheim:** the glass headquarters on a suburban business park outside Munich (real Commons photo); Munich.
- **Frankfurt:** the stock exchange, its bull and bear statues, the DAX board.
- **The Financial Times:**
  - its salmon-pink newsprint (the paper's own colour);
  - the "House of Wirecard" series by Dan McCrum (real article screenshots of the cited sources);
  - the FT masthead never copied, only the paper's colour and column type.
- **BaFin:** the regulator's building in Bonn / Frankfurt (Commons).
- **EY:** the audit (a stamped audit opinion, a signature withheld).
- **The Philippines:** two banks (BDO, BPI), their confirmation letters called "spurious" (a forged document), Manila.
- **People:** Markus Braun (black turtleneck, the tech-visionary pose: real press photos), Jan Marsalek (the wanted
  notice photo), the Prinzregentenstraße villa.
- **The escape:** a private jet, Minsk, cash.

## The world in one line
The empire was a stream of transactions that said "APPROVED". The film is printed on its own receipts: thermal paper,
terminal light and the FT's pink paper that kept asking where the money was.

## Its motifs
- **The receipt.** Numbers are printed line by line on thermal paper as he says them. The film's figures are
  transaction lines: €24 BILLION as a total line, €1.9 BILLION as a balance that prints, then prints "NOT FOUND".
- **The terminal LCD.** One word on a small green-grey screen ("APPROVED"), held: the company's voice. At the fall it
  says "DECLINED".
- **The pink paper.** Every scene about the FT investigation sits on salmon-pink newsprint texture. The reporter's
  questions are typeset in the paper's column, and real article screenshots are laid on the pink.
- **Glass.** The Aschheim HQ's glass façade: reflections, the company seen through glass.

## How money, power and collapse are shown
- **Money** is never banknotes raining. It is transaction totals printing, and a terminal approving.
- **Power** is the DAX board and the bull statue in Frankfurt, and Braun's pose in the turtleneck.
- **Collapse** is the receipt tearing off, the terminal declining, the confirmation letter shown as a forgery (stamp
  "SPURIOUS"), the €1.9 billion line printing "NOT FOUND".
- **Escape** is cash in the counting machine, the jet, a departures board to Minsk.

## Word device
Key words as thermal-print type (a monospaced receipt face): they print line by line on the voice, slightly faded at
the edges like real thermal paper, and stay until read. On the FT's pink, words are set in a newspaper column serif.
No glow, no gradient fill.

## Transition
The receipt tear: the frame tears along a perforated edge and the next picture is underneath, about 10 frames.
Also an LCD refresh (a scanline wipe of the terminal's screen) inside the company's own scenes.

## Palette
- thermal-paper white-grey;
- the terminal's green-grey LCD;
- FT salmon pink;
- Wirecard blue;
- deep shadows.

No cyan steel, no red warning wash.
