# Why the Nokia proof film is slop — gap analysis against MagnatesMedia (2026-11-23, corrected by the owner)

Film: `out/bl4c-nokia/` (tag `platform-frozen-bl4.2`), 92 s, 16:9. Evidence:
- the film itself, one frame every 1.6 s;
- 30 random real MagnatesMedia shots (`data/exemplars/magnates-media`);
- the film's own reports: film-score, preflight, QA, viewer, final-cut judge, narration, sound.

Its scores:

| Measure | Ours | Channel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Film score | 77/100 | — |
| Trained taste | 4.1 | median 6.1, lowest tenth 4.4 |
| Final-cut judge picked it out as the imitation | 6 of 11 stretches | 44 % for a real stretch |

**The verdict in one line:** the film reads as a summary narrated over a slide deck, because each agent works below
the reference's own craft. The owner's standard (2026-11-23) is that length is no excuse: a film of a few seconds,
10 minutes or 20 minutes must be made at the reference's level, in any style, fast, cheap and without stopping. Every
gap below is a craft gap in a named agent, plus one budget fault that kept the agents from working at all.

## 2. Picture: what the frames show

### 3.1 One template carries half the film

- About 30 of the 60 sampled frames sit on the same backdrop: navy gradient, faint grid, orange ember particles, orange
  glow along the bottom. Cards, charts, portrait frames and phrase boxes all sit on it.
- **The channel never does this.** Its 30 real shots show 30 different worlds: a red sunburst behind a LEGO box, a sepia
  battlefield with stencil type, a black-and-white boardroom with a green logo plate, a Coca-Cola ad page, a red Netflix
  icon card, a 3D "ROLLS ROYCE" over the building, a terminal UI with brackets.
- The judge named it in 5 of 6 picked stretches: "dark blue grid with orange ember particles… looks like a generic
  corporate/tech template".
- The kit has one stage component and the scene designer used it for every shot without footage. The channel's
  "stage" is almost never a stage: it is a picture treated (graded, cut out, collaged) or a bold flat colour field that
  changes with each idea.
- Measured:
  - designed shots: 62–80 % against the channel's 30 %;
  - stock footage: 25 % against 49 %;
  - text on screen: 20–25 % of shots against 52 %.

  The channel carries words on top of pictures. We carry words and charts on an empty stage.

### 3.2 Charts held as slides

- Five bar charts:
  - Q4 2007 volumes;
  - Nokia vs Samsung;
  - smartphone share, twice;
  - deal price.
- All five are the same template: flat blue and orange bars with tiny labels, held 4–7 s with nothing moving after the
  build.
- The channel's data (in the sample) is a single number or a line drawn with glow on a textured ground, a hexagon
  share icon, and an annotated picture ("STRATEGY → MARKETING" over a Coke bottle). It is rarely a plain bar chart, and
  never five.
- The last picture of the film is a chart (€3.8B / €1.6B) while the voice says "5.44 billion euros". The viewer flagged
  it, and the two figures do not visibly add up to what is said.

### 3.3 Phrase cards instead of type in the picture

- "END UP ON FIRE?", "PHASE OUT SYMBIAN", "WINDOWS PHONE", "SAMSUNG OVERTOOK NOKIA" and "SOLD ITS PHONE BUSINESS" are each
  small orange-boxed labels on the empty stage, each held 3–5 s. Three of them repeat over two or three shots.
- The channel's words are huge, set into the picture and part of the composition:
  - "FRANCE WAS ONCE AGAIN HEADING TO WAR" stencilled over smoke;
  - "HAVE YOUR TWO CENTS…" filling a red field;
  - "EVENTUALLY EVEN START A VIDEO GAME DIVISION" next to the LEGO box.
- Our cards are about 3 % of the frame. Theirs are 20–50 % and integrated.

### 3.4 Pictures: real, but used weakly

- **Real material, used once and then repeated:**
  - Elop's portrait appears in 4 shots, framed the same way, as a photo card on the stage;
  - the Nokia–Microsoft handshake is the one strong press moment;
  - the 3310, the iPhone on stage and the Lumia box.
- **Generic stock where a picture of the story was needed:**
  - an empty modern office;
  - a hand writing on paper;
  - a woman's hands holding a modern phone;
  - a man typing on an old Nokia slider.

  They are lit like stock, graded like stock and held like stock (4–6 s).
- **Wrong or meaningless pictures:**
  - a rainbow-lit London night facade (for "a conference in London") reads as a nightclub;
  - a 3310 lying on a dragon-patterned plate.
- **A visible defect:** the Lumia box carries a cut-off "MTR" watermark.
- **Nothing is treated:**
  - no cut-outs over backgrounds;
  - no grade that ties the shots together;
  - no paper texture, film grain or light leaks;
  - no collage.

  The channel's frames almost all carry one of these: a black-and-white grade with a coloured element, a cut-out over a
  burst, a vignette with grain.

### 3.5 Motion and editing

- **Median shot 3.6 s** against the channel's 2.0 s (its films run 1.5–2.6 s). Shot lengths barely vary: 0.39 against
  1.0–1.8.
- **Push-ins on the same picture with no transition** (office, London, Lumia, the hand with a phone). They read as
  jump cuts or a stutter.
- **Cuts land exactly on word boundaries** (distance 0 against the channel's ~0.07 s), and a third of them fall in
  voice pauses (against 11 %). The cut follows the script mechanically instead of the picture.
- **No hit lands on a cut** (0 % against 15–37 %).
- **Camera:** a slow zoom on everything; no whips, no parallax, no speed ramps, no match cuts. The kit's transition
  (an orange wipe) is used twice.

### 3.6 Look and colour

- Too dark: luma 0.198 against the channel's 0.233–0.321.
- Too saturated and too blue-orange: colourfulness 49.7 against 31–46.
- No unifying grade: the stock shots keep their own looks (cold office, rainbow London, warm hands).

## 3. Script: summary, not story

The full narration is 7 sentences, 250 words, on the "how it went wrong" arc.

- **No scene.** The channel opens on a moment ("It's 1998 and…", a person in a place doing something). We open on a quote
  and immediately explain it. A good writer finds a scene even in 90 seconds: one moment, one person, one place.
- **No character.** Elop is a name and a title. Nobody wants, fears or decides anything we can see. Jorma Ollila, the
  engineers and Ballmer are all missing.
- **Narrator talk instead of hooks:**
  - "It sounds absurd, because…";
  - "This is the story of…";
  - "In other words…".
- **Telescoped chronology:** the iPhone (2007) and Elop's appointment (2010) are in one sentence, so the three-year
  denial, which is the actual story, is gone.
- **Vague facts:** "the night before a conference in London the final decision was made" (which conference? what
  decision moment?). "Heat from competitors" is a paraphrase of the memo, whose real images (the burning platform, the
  man jumping into icy water) are the channel's kind of material and go unused.
- **The ending restates the opening** ("the platform had burned, just as the memo said") instead of landing a
  consequence or a twist.
- **The story band passed** (`script.story.json`). The band measures line kinds and length, not whether there is a
  story. That measure is too weak to stop this.

## 4. Voice (judged on sounding human; the voice keeps its own natural pace, never the reference narrator's)

- **Delivery sounds read, not told.** The voice director's own notes stay open after its retakes:
  - statements end rising, so they sound unsure or like a list (lines 1 and 6);
  - the stress on "danger", "bet" and "burned" does not land, so the lines that should hit go flat.

  It ran out of budget and never submitted ("not submitted").
- **Mistakes a listener hears:** "Elop" is still wrong in one line, and "non-Finnish" was heard as "finished".
- **Stops inside lines.** The voice pauses inside sentences where a person telling a story would run on, which breaks
  the thought and sounds read. This is judged on how it sounds, not counted against the reference narrator.
- **Voice character:** the "optimistic storyteller" colour sits against a story of collapse. A human narrator shifts
  tone across a story (dry, then grave, then wry), and ours stays in one colour.

## 5. Sound

- **One music bed** for 92 s, with no change at the turn (iPhone arrives) or the fall (Samsung overtakes). The channel
  changes cue at every act and drops music out for a hit.
- **Six effects**, 67 % of them used by a recent film (`shared_sfx` failed), and none on a cut.
- **No riser into the climax, no drop-out before the sale, no silence used as punctuation.**

## 6. Packaging

- The thumbnail is the best part: a real 3310 in real fire over a real Nokia HQ, which the channel would plausibly make.
  It has no words, though, and fire on a phone says "exploding phone", not "lost the war".
- The title is the topic sentence, "How Nokia lost the smartphone war". The channel's titles are sharper and promise a
  person or a twist.

## 7. Why the platform let this through

| Gap | What the platform checks | Why it passed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One template on half the film | `unique_look` (against other films), `film_taste` | Nothing measures sameness of backdrop *within* a film; taste failed (4.1 < 4.4) but the gate was starved |
| Bar-chart slides | `fact_binding`, `number_parity` | They check numbers, not whether a chart is the right way to show them or how long it holds |
| Phrase cards | lone-word guard (bl4) | Two- and three-word labels pass; nothing measures text size or integration with the picture |
| Generic stock | picture review fit ≥ 6/10 | "A woman holding a phone" fits "almost everyone buying Nokia smartphones had gone elsewhere" literally; fit is not meaning |
| Summary script | story band | It measures line kinds, not scenes, character or chronology |
| Voice | delivery notices, WER | Notices are advisory, and the voice agent ran out of budget |
| Sound | sound score 79 → 93 | Scored inside its own rules (hit count), not against how the channel scores a story |

## 8. The gaps, ranked by how much they cost the film

1. **Budget starvation** (fixed in bl4.3, unproven): no agent below can act without it.

2. **Story:** no scene, no character, telescoped time, narrator filler. The writer needs a scene-and-character
   test, not a line-kind band.
3. **Template stage:** one backdrop for half the film. A designed shot must be built from a picture (treated,
   cut out, collaged) or a bold field that changes with the idea, and the same backdrop must be limited within a film.
4. **Pictures:** generic stock and repeats. Rate by meaning (does it show the *idea* of the line?) and ban stock people
   doing nothing; use each portrait once; treat every picture (grade, cut-out, texture).
5. **Charts:** five slides. At most one chart per minute; prefer one number, a drawn line or an annotated picture, and
   no chart held longer than 3 s without movement.
6. **Type:** tiny boxed labels. Words must be big and set into the picture, and a phrase card must never repeat over
   shots.
7. **Edit:** long, even shots and jump push-ins. Aim for a median shot of about 2 s with varied lengths, cuts off the
   word boundary, and hits on cuts.
8. **Voice:** rising statements, flat stresses, pauses inside sentences and one colour for the whole story. The voice
   director must hear and fix these, without changing the pace.
9. **Sound:** one bed and shared effects. Change the cue at each act, use drop-outs and a riser, and use fresh effects.
10. **Look:** too dark, too saturated, no unifying grade. The colourist must run.
